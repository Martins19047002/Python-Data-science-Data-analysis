# Research Hypotheses for the Indiana COVID-19 Project

## Hypothesis 1: Distinct epidemic waves existed in Indiana

Indiana’s COVID-19 trends likely showed multiple wave-like periods, with elevated daily case counts over specific phases of the pandemic. This hypothesis is grounded in the broader pattern of epidemic dynamics observed nationally and globally.

## Hypothesis 2: Vaccination rollout affected severe disease outcomes

As vaccination coverage increased, severe outcomes such as deaths and hospitalization burden may have declined relative to earlier outbreak phases. This is a key public health question in evaluating the impact of vaccination programs.

## Hypothesis 3: Testing volume and positivity rate shaped observed case patterns

Increased testing volume may have revealed more infections, but positivity rate helps determine whether the testing system was detecting widespread transmission or whether results were influenced by testing volume alone.

## Hypothesis 4: Case trends and mortality trends did not move in a perfectly synchronized way

Observed case counts and deaths may follow different time patterns because of reporting lag, healthcare capacity, treatment improvements, behavior change, and fluctuating transmission intensity over time.

## Suggested analysis methods

- Line plots for daily cases, deaths, positivity, and vaccination trend
- 7-day moving averages to smooth short-term noise
- Correlation checks between testing volume and case counts
- Descriptive comparison across major pandemic phases

## Expected interpretation

If the hypotheses are supported, the project will show that Indiana’s COVID-19 experience was shaped by multiple overlapping factors, including wave dynamics, testing infrastructure, vaccination, and public health response.
