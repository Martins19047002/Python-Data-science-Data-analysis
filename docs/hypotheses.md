# Research Hypotheses for the Indiana COVID-19 Project

## Hypothesis 1: Indiana experienced multiple epidemic waves

Indiana likely showed multiple periods of elevated case counts across the observed period. This is consistent with broader pandemic patterns in the United States, where infections rose and fell in waves tied to behavioral change, policy interventions, and immunity.

## Hypothesis 2: Vaccination rollout is associated with lower severe outcomes

As vaccination coverage increased, the state may have experienced a relative reduction in severe health outcomes compared with earlier outbreak phases. This is a key hypothesis for evaluating the public health impact of vaccine rollout.

## Hypothesis 3: Testing volume and positivity are informative indicators

Higher testing volume may reveal more infections, but positivity rate helps clarify whether the increase reflects actual transmission intensity or simply more testing. Together, these variables help explain observed case statistics.

## Hypothesis 4: Case and mortality trends do not always align perfectly

Case counts and death counts may move at different speeds because of reporting lag, changes in healthcare response, treatment improvements, and shifting transmission conditions. This informs how the project interprets time-series data.

## Analysis approach

- Plot daily cases and deaths over time
- Calculate 7-day moving averages to smooth short-term fluctuations
- Review positivity and vaccination trends
- Compare broad phases of the pandemic and summarize pattern changes

## Interpretation goal

This project aims to show that COVID-19 in Indiana was shaped by a combination of social, biological, and public health factors. The analyses should not treat the pandemic as a single linear trend, but as a dynamic process with multiple phases.
